# Animation Principles (Disney's 12 Principles of Animation)

Apply Ollie Johnston & Frank Thomas's **12 Principles of Animation** (*The Illusion of Life*) whenever designing, blocking, animating, or reviewing motion in **3D previs (Blender / three.js)**, **hand-painted animation**, **AI video prompts (Gemini Omni Flash / Veo / Seedance)**, or **interactive motion**.

## The 12 Principles — Production Quick Reference

| # | Principle | Motion / Visual Role | Previs & AI Video Application |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Squash & Stretch** | Conveys mass, weight, and flexibility while preserving volume | Subtle elastic compression/expansion on creatures, water impact, lantern flame, and emotional weight |
| 2 | **Anticipation** | Prepares the viewer's eye before the main action fires | Brief stillness, breath, shoulder stiffen, or wind-up beat before a sudden surge, bloom, or camera reveal |
| 3 | **Staging** | Directs attention unmistakably to ONE idea per shot | Silhouette test in solid black; clear foreground/midground/background separation; zero visual clutter behind the focal subject |
| 4 | **Straight Ahead vs Pose to Pose** | Spontaneity (organic elements) vs choreographed precision (key beats) | Pose-to-pose for locked camera beats & cut endpoints; straight-ahead feel for drifting petals, spray, mist, and water ripples |
| 5 | **Follow Through & Overlapping Action** | Nothing stops all at once; appendages & environment trail the core body | Whale pectoral fins & tail fluke lag behind torso turns; cloaks, water spray, and petals settle AFTER the whale passes |
| 6 | **Slow In & Slow Out** | Natural acceleration and deceleration — never robotic linear speed | Bezier easing on cameras & organic arcs; tighter spacing near holds and landing poses ("cushion") |
| 7 | **Arc** | Living creatures and cinematic cameras move along smooth curves | Curved swimming paths, banking glides, head turns, and crane/orbital camera arcs instead of straight lines |
| 8 | **Secondary Action** | Supporting motion that enriches without competing with the primary beat | Lantern flame flicker, trembling dew drops, drifting 3-layer floral snow, subtle adventurer weight shift |
| 9 | **Timing** | Frame count defines weight, scale, and mood | Huge 24m creatures move with slow, deliberate, weighty timing; tiny petals drift softly; silence & holds amplify awe |
| 10 | **Exaggeration** | Calibrated push beyond flat realism for clarity and genre tone | Restrained ~10% theatrical push for poetic anime/cinema (never cartoonish squash on solemn beats) |
| 11 | **Solid Drawing** | Consistent 3D volume, anatomy, perspective, and spatial coherence | Avoid dead "twin" symmetry unless deliberate; maintain 180° rule, screen direction, and exact proportions across cuts |
| 12 | **Appeal** | Charisma in silhouette, rhythm, and craft that holds the viewer | Graceful, readable silhouettes and deliberate pacing that makes every frame earn its screen time |

## Director's Review Checklist (Run Before Rendering or Video Generation)

1. **Silhouette Test (Staging + Solid Drawing)**: If filled solid black, is the subject's pose, direction, and scale immediately readable?
2. **Weight & Scale Check (Timing + Slow In/Out)**: Does the speed match the subject's physical mass (e.g., 24m whale = slow majestic momentum, zero sudden linear snaps)?
3. **Preparation & Settle (Anticipation + Follow Through)**: Does each major turn/reveal have a preparatory beat before it and an overlapping settle after it?
4. **Living Layer (Secondary Action + Arc)**: Does every held shot include one motivated curved secondary layer (mist, petals, ripples, flame)?
5. **Emotional Clarity (Appeal + Exaggeration)**: Does every movement advance the beat's emotion rather than showing off technical motion?
