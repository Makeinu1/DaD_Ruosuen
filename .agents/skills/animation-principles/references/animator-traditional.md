# Traditional Animator: Classical Animation Craft

> Source: [dylantarre/animation-principles — skills/03-by-role-persona/animator-traditional/SKILL.md](https://github.com/dylantarre/animation-principles/blob/main/skills/03-by-role-persona/animator-traditional/SKILL.md) (MIT License)

1. **Squash and Stretch** — Illusion of weight and flexibility with constant volume.
2. **Anticipation** — Preparation for action; audiences read the preparation before the move.
3. **Staging** — Present one idea unmistakably clear. **Silhouette test**: can you read the pose in solid black?
4. **Straight Ahead vs Pose to Pose** — Straight ahead for fire, water, wind, petals; pose to pose for acting and choreography; masters combine both.
5. **Follow Through and Overlapping Action** — Nothing stops at once; drag on appendages and settle after main action stops.
6. **Slow In and Slow Out** — More drawings/tighter spacing near poses ("cushion"), fewer in fast motion.
7. **Arc** — Natural movement follows curved paths; breaking arcs feels mechanical unless deliberate.
8. **Secondary Action** — Subsidiary actions supporting the main action without competing with it.
9. **Timing** — Frame count defines weight, scale, and mood (slow timing = large/heavy; snappy = small/light).
10. **Exaggeration** — Amplification of the essence of an action for clarity, calibrated to style.
11. **Solid Drawing** — True 3D form, weight, and volume; avoid dead symmetrical "twin" poses unless intentional.
12. **Appeal** — Strong silhouettes, pleasing proportions, charismatic motion.

## Traditional Workflow
1. Thumbnails → 2. Keys → 3. Breakdowns (arc & timing) → 4. In-betweens → 5. Clean-up → 6. Flip, shoot, review.

Mantra: *"Does it feel alive? Does it have weight? Does it have thought? Does it have appeal?"*
